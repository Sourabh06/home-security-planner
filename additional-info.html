Tougher cameras: IP and IK ratings

IP rating: dust and waterTwo digits. The first is dust protection, the second is water protection. Higher is better.
IP66 is dust-tight and survives heavy rain and strong water jets. It is commonly treated as the minimum for outdoors.
IP67 also survives a short dip in water.
Outdoor cameras
IK rating: knocks and vandalsHow hard a hit the camera body can take, from IK00 to IK10.
IK10 withstands 20 joules, like a 5 kg weight dropped from 40 cm. That makes it vandal-resistant, but not unbreakable.
Cameras within arm's reach

These ratings mostly appear on higher-end cameras and cost more. The prices in this planner don't say which ratings your cameras have, so check the spec sheet.

Buying in India: look for STQC

Government security checkSince 1 April 2026, new internet-connected (IP or network) CCTV cameras and recorders sold in India must be BIS-registered and pass security testing by STQC, a government testing body under MeitY. The test looks at things like secure software updates and locked-down video streams.

Ask the seller for: the BIS registration number, and the STQC (ER-01) certificate for that exact model.
Mandatory in IndiaRules can change, so confirm before you buy

Battery cameras

A battery camera has no power cable. You recharge it, or pair it with a small solar panel. It is not part of the cost estimate in this planner, so add its price yourself if you plan to use one.

  • Easy to place: no cable to lay, so it suits a gate, shed or rented home.
  • Clips, not non-stop video: to save power, most wake up on movement and save a short clip, so they may miss what happens between clips.
  • Recharging: battery life depends on how often the camera triggers. Busy spots drain it faster.
  • Connection: most still need WiFi, so a weak signal means patchy video. A few use a SIM card instead (see below).

SIM card and SD card options

Only a few cameras support a SIM card or an SD card. Check the spec sheet for the exact model before you buy. The cameras priced in this planner may not have either option.

SD card (microSD)The camera saves video on a memory card inside it, so it can record without a DVR or NVR. The card is small and can be stolen with the camera. It is not priced here.
SIM card (4G)The camera uses mobile data instead of WiFi or a network cable. It is useful where there is no broadband. You need a data plan, and it is not priced here.

Alarms and event detection

A camera can do more than record. It can raise an alert when something happens:

  • Movement: something moves in the picture.
  • Line or zone: someone crosses a line you drew or steps into a marked area.
  • Person or vehicle: smart models ignore swaying trees and passing animals.
  • Sound: a loud noise, such as breaking glass, on cameras with a microphone.
  • Tampering: the camera is covered or turned away.

An alert can send a message to your phone, save a short clip, or switch on a siren or light. Shadows, insects and rain can cause false alarms. The features depend on the model, and sirens and alarm devices are not priced in this planner.

Image recognition (smart detection)

Some cameras and recorders use AI to understand the picture, not just spot movement. They can tell a person from a vehicle or an animal, read number plates, or match faces against a saved list. This may happen inside the camera or recorder, or in a cloud service. Models with it built in usually cost more, so ask where the processing happens.

Privacy and safety concerns

  • Aim with care: cover only your own door, gate and rooms. Keep bedrooms, bathrooms, neighbours' windows and staff quarters out of view.
  • No secret recording: tell people cameras are in use, for example with a simple sign.
  • Faces and number plates are sensitive: face matching is the most personal feature, so use it only if you really need it.
  • Cloud or local: footage in a cloud service sits on someone else's server. Recording locally keeps it at home.
  • Keep it secure: change default passwords, keep updates on, and keep cameras off your main home network if you can.
  • Do not rely on alarms alone: in an emergency, call the police or emergency services. A camera alert is not a response.

In India, footage of an identifiable person is personal data under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Ordinary household use may be treated differently from a shop or housing society, but being careful is good practice either way. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

DORI: how clearly will the camera see?

DORI comes from the IEC EN 62676-4 standard. It rates a camera by how many pixels land on each metre of the scene (PPM). More pixels per metre means more detail.

Detect25 PPM
Someone is there.
Observe63 PPM
What they wear and do.
Recognize125 PPM
Whether it is someone you know.
Identify250 PPM
Who a stranger is.

Two things decide how far a camera reaches each level: the picture width in pixels (more MP is better) and the view angle (a narrower view reaches farther). It is the same idea as the View angle slider on the 3D page. The closer the person, the more detail you get.

These are planning values, not guarantees. Light, focus and movement also change what you can see.
